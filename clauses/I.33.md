## Clause

Every person has the right to know whether the media they encounter was generated
or materially altered by an artificial system, and to have that disclosure
presented at the point of encounter rather than buried in metadata.

This right may not be waived by contract.

## Rationale

Synthetic media is now indistinguishable from recorded media at the point of
perception. Disclosure buried in metadata satisfies a compliance requirement
without informing anyone, which is worse than no rule at all — it creates the
appearance of protection while leaving people exactly as deceived.

The non-waivable provision exists because a right that can be signed away in
terms of service is not a right. It is a default.

## Notes

"Materially altered" is intended to exclude ordinary correction — colour, crop,
noise reduction — and to include anything that changes what a reasonable person
would take the media to depict.

Cross-references I.4 (transparency of systems), I.12 (right to an unmediated
account), and II.4 (enforcement and redress).
