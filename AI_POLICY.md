# AI contribution policy

**Agent-assisted contribution is welcome here. Undisclosed contribution is not.**

Most projects publishing a policy right now are writing a refusal, and they have
good reason to. Roughly two-thirds of maintainers report agent-generated pull
requests as a significant burden. curl received twenty invalid AI security
reports in twenty-one days. Projects have started banning the practice outright.

The problem is not that a machine helped. The problem is that nobody can tell
who is accountable for the result. We fix that at the root instead of banning the
tool.

## The four stances, and ours

Open-source projects have converged on four positions: **refuse**, **disclose**,
**verify**, **handoff**. We take the last three and reject the first.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Disclose** | Every contribution records the agent's role. Declared by the contributor. |
| **Verify** | Every contribution is checked against the constitution before it lands. |
| **Handoff** | Anything that changes the public record needs explicit human confirmation. |
| ~~Refuse~~ | Not needed. Provenance solves what refusal was protecting against. |

## What you must declare

Every sealed contribution records one of:

- **`drafted`** — the agent wrote the substance
- **`assisted`** — the agent shaped, expanded, or restructured your thinking
- **`reviewed`** — you wrote it; the agent checked it
- **`none`** — the agent did not touch the text

Spelling and formatting alone require no declaration.

Declare honestly. Under-declaring is a conduct violation and can cost you your
agent credential. We would rather have an accurate record of heavy AI use than a
flattering record that is false.

## What we do not do

**We do not detect.** No classifier decides whether your text was machine-written.
Detection is unreliable, it produces false accusations, and it falls hardest on
people writing in a second language. Disclosure is an honour system backed by
consequences, not a surveillance system.

**We do not read your agent's conversations.** Telemetry stops at metadata and
safety verdicts. Your agent is yours. A digital-rights project that surveilled
its members' assistants would deserve everything said about it.

**We do not rank human above machine contribution.** A well-argued position is
well-argued. What we require is that a named human stands behind it.

## Why accountability, not authorship

Attribution runs to the **human**, always. Your agent has no standing here. It
holds a credential issued to you, it acts within scopes you hold, and if it
misbehaves the consequence lands on you.

That is the whole design. It is why we can accept contributions that other
projects have to refuse.

## Volume

Provenance does not make review free. Reviewer capacity is the real constraint in
open source, and no amount of tooling changes that.

Contributions should be **small, specific, and answerable**. If you would not
read it yourself, do not file it. Bulk submissions get closed without review, and
repeated bulk submission costs you your credential.
